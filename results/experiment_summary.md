# Experiment summary

Every number below is regenerated by `code/run_all.sh`. The figures come from the run of 11 September 2026 (statsmodels 0.14.6, scikit-learn 1.8.0, LightGBM 4.7.0, SHAP 0.51.0).

Protocol: rolling origin, three origins (train 2015-2022 to test 2023, then 2015-2023 to test 2024, then 2015-2024 to test 2025), one step ahead each time. Every model sees identical windows and identical inputs within a feature set. The ML target is log1p of the value and all models are scored in levels (EUR) after back-transformation. Seeds fixed at 42. All 15 PPML fits converged, in 9 to 15 iterations: 6 model fits plus the 9 expanding-window fits that build the hybrid feature.

Panel: 277,992 rows (39 suppliers x 27 destinations x 24 HS6 codes x 11 years), 72,288 of them nonzero, so 74.0% zeros, EUR 9,024.5M in total, heading 5806 accounting for 27.8%.

## RQ2 - Coefficients against importances (Table 5.3)

PPML spec A, origin O3, standard errors clustered by exporter-destination pair: ln GDP_o **+0.700** (p<0.001), ln GDP_d **+0.835** (p<0.001), ln dist **-0.393** (p=0.010, weak, which echoes Chen et al.), contig +1.155 (n.s.), comlang_off +0.536 (n.s.), comcol -1.402 (p<0.001), rta **-0.701** (p=0.002). The negative RTA sign is counterintuitive and is discussed rather than explained away: the EU's largest trimmings suppliers have no agreement with it, so the dummy picks up selection.

Permutation importance on the winner, O3 test set: roll3_log **0.321** and l1_log **0.147** dominate, which is persistence the gravity structure cannot see. The best economic variable is ln_gdp_o at 0.029, then zero_streak 0.018, ln_gdp_d 0.008, ln_pop_o 0.008, ln_dist 0.003.

SHAP values are computed on LGBM_lags, because TreeExplainer on a forest this size is not feasible and LGBM is the runner-up with the same features. Mean absolute SHAP: roll3_log 1.251, l1_log 0.456, ln_gdp_o 0.328, zero_streak 0.315, ln_gdp_d 0.246, ln_dist 0.135. Directions: ln_gdp_d +0.071 and ln_dist **+0.178**. Distance comes out positive in the ML against a negative PPML coefficient, a real divergence: once persistence is controlled, distance proxies the large Asian suppliers.

## RQ3 - Brazil (Table 5.4, test years 2023 to 2025 pooled)

- Benchmark RF_lags: Brazil observed EUR 12.65M against EUR 10.88M predicted, **+16.3%**.
- The ten suppliers nearest to Brazil in cumulative EU imports show a median of **+59.9%**, range +17.0% to +174.1%. Brazil ranks **11th of 11**, last of its peer group.
- PPML_B as the cross-check: Brazil **-13.5%**. The two benchmarks disagree on the sign of the gap but agree on the comparative reading, which is the one the dissertation uses.
- By heading: 5806, which is 98.7% of Brazil's EU trade, comes out at +15.0% (12.486 observed vs 10.861 predicted). The tiny headings show very large relative deviations off near-zero bases, a base effect that is flagged rather than interpreted.
- By destination: Romania absorbs almost the whole flow and sits at +10.7% under the winner, while PPML_B puts it at +2,111.8% because the gravity model expects the Brazilian volume to spread across the larger member states. See `rq3_brazil_by_destination.csv` and `rq3_brazil_by_destination_ppml.csv`.

A deviation mixes unrealized potential, model error and omitted factors. It is an indicative signal, not an entry recommendation.

| 5b ablation: no rta | 107,625 | 318,634 | Y |
| 6 EU as one bloc | 1,016,681 | 2,420,797 | Y |

Eight checks, and the one that fails is the informative one. Strip the lags and the forest loses to PPML, which is the same point the ablation in Table 5.2 makes: the ML gain comes from persistence and not from a better use of the gravity covariates. A set of checks that all confirmed would deserve more suspicion than this one.

## Real-world validation

Mirror of Brazil to the EU27, Comext against ComexStat: mean discrepancy +6.1% in the expected direction (CIF above FOB), correlation 0.985. The pandemic shows up where it should, with -20.1% in 2020 and a +18.1% rebound in 2021. Details in `validation_report.md`.
